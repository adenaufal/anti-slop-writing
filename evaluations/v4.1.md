# v4.1 language-editing evaluation

**Date:** September 27, 2026. **Baseline release:** v4.0 (`c282e47`). This record describes checks of editing instructions, not a measurement of provider-specific writing styles or a comparison with unassisted output.

## Reproducible material

- [Eight requests](v4.1-cases.json): four Indonesian and four English scenarios. All names, counts, and events are synthetic test material.
- [Sixteen actual outputs](v4.1-outputs.json): separate Full and Lite runs, generated by independent `gpt-6-luna` agents at medium reasoning. Each agent received the requests and the relevant instructions, without an answer key or findings from the other run. Full could load its language references; Lite was tested without Full or references.
- [English repairs](../english/references/language-editing.md) and [Indonesian repairs](../indonesian/references/language-editing.md): constructed examples with retention conditions. They are not quotations from Claude, Gemini, Grok, or a provider-wide corpus.

JSON completeness, the listed numbers/dates/qualifiers/register terms, and the three-bullet counts were checked programmatically. The revised meanings, modifier attachment, and ambiguity handling were also reviewed directly against the requests. String checks alone do not establish semantic equivalence. These small cases support the listed behaviors; they do not establish an error rate, prove improvement over v4.0, or cover every rule.

Review of the instructional examples found and corrected meaning drift: `some` becoming `a few`, an unnamed actor becoming `I`, an invented object in a parallel list, an unsupported contrast added by `but`, and a passive recording action incorrectly assigned to the testing team. Those are review findings about the proposed examples, not provider statistics.

## Research basis and limits

[Sourati et al. (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02550-0) report that rewriting can compress differences in writing complexity in their tested English texts and systems. The studied rewriting models include GPT-3.5, Gemini, and Llama 3; publication in 2026 does not establish findings for the September 2026 model families. The editorial inference used here is to protect the supplied writer's voice and change only what the editing goal requires.

[Crosthwaite, Sun, and Jiang (2026)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/modl.70089) study how prompt specificity changes metadiscourse in GPT-generated argumentative essays. The editorial inference is to check the function of a transition or hedge in the actual draft, rather than label it a fixed model habit. [Yen and Chung (2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.rocling-main.38/) examine the roles of `however` and `while` in a small argumentative corpus; the study does not prescribe removing these words from all prose.

[WMT25 multilingual instruction findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.23/) provide broader task and language evidence, not validated Indonesian grammar rules or a current Grok/Claude/Gemini fingerprint. Indonesian spelling and punctuation rules in the guide cite [official EYD V](https://ejaan.kemendikdasmen.go.id/); English conventional grammar repairs cite Purdue OWL. Sentence-level choices and constructed examples remain contextual editorial guidance.

## Direct provider access

Two attempts used only synthetic writing prompts in an isolated temporary directory. Neither produced a model output:

- Claude Code, latest `fable` alias: selected model was unavailable or inaccessible (API status 404). No successful Claude generation was recorded.
- Gemini CLI, `gemini-3.8-flash`: authentication was rejected because the installed client was unsupported for the account. No successful Gemini generation was recorded.

No Grok generation interface was available. The successful OpenAI runs above used Codex's `gpt-6-luna` agents; they were skill-guided editing tests, not ordinary ChatGPT defaults. No controlled comparison of the four latest provider families was completed, and no provider-specific linguistic frequencies are claimed. The v4.0 model-release snapshot is retained as historical reference.

## Packaging and orchestration

Use `python scripts/build_skills.py` from the repository to validate and build the four archives; `python scripts/build_skills.py --check` verifies existing archives without writing them. The helper checks local links, Full/adapter equality, version consistency, installation Python syntax, ZIP integrity, entry sets, and exact source bytes. The skill-creator frontmatter validator is run separately during release preparation. These checks establish file and package consistency, not literary quality.

The four frontmatter validations passed. All six adapters were synchronized with the Full bodies. The four v4.1 ZIP archives passed exact-entry, integrity, and source-byte verification (9 entries per Full archive, 6 per Lite archive). `--check` also succeeded when invoked from outside the repository. `git diff --check` and the build helper's Python syntax check passed. The historical model references match v4.0, and the v4.0 tag still points to `c282e47`. This evaluation concluded before publication; see the [v4.1 release](https://github.com/adenaufal/anti-slop-writing/releases/tag/v4.1) for the published packages and release history.

Routing used Luna medium for language work and independent editing scenarios, and Luna low for the deterministic packaging helper. Jev was not run because `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` was absent. The remaining editorial judgments were reviewed directly; no unavailable judgment or failed provider attempt is reported as a pass.
