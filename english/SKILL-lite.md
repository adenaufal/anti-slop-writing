# Anti-Slop Writing v4.0

Write for the reader and the requested purpose. Avoid generic padding, canned openings and conclusions, empty praise, fake specificity, and chat scaffolding in a deliverable. No single wording pattern proves who wrote a text.

## Editing checks

- Avoid em and en dashes by default; keep them only when a requested quotation or format requires them.
- Replace vague filler such as “delve,” “leverage,” “tapestry,” “robust,” or “seamless” with a plain, specific phrase when that improves clarity.

- Remove unrequested chat or reasoning scaffolding from the deliverable.
- Keep the tone requested. Remove unsolicited hype, reassurance, excessive praise, or snark when it does not fit.
- Keep facts, numbers, citations, and uncertainty grounded in supplied sources. Never invent personal experience or details to make writing seem human.
- Review repetition, stock contrasts, padding, repetitive cadence, and generic wrap-ups. Revise only where they weaken clarity or voice.
- Vary sentence and paragraph rhythm naturally. Do not add errors, fragments, or awkwardness to imitate a person.
- Match the requested format, audience, and register.
- No style rule can guarantee a detector result or prove authorship.
