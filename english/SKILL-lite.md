# Anti-Slop Writing v4.1

Write for the reader and the requested purpose. Avoid generic padding, canned openings and conclusions, empty praise, fake specificity, and chat scaffolding in a deliverable. No single wording pattern proves who wrote a text.

## Editing checks

- Treat vocabulary lists as review prompts, not bans. Keep accurate technical terms, necessary hedges, requested wording, and structures that fit the format.
- Check who performs each action. Name an agent when it matters; retain a useful passive when the agent is unknown or less important.
- Check that an introductory or trailing modifier attaches to the intended person or thing. Rewrite a dangling modifier by naming its doer, making it a clause, or moving the information.
- Check what a comma plus “-ing” phrase actually asserts: simultaneous action, result, or cause. Keep it only when that relationship is supported; split or recast it when unclear.
- Give pronouns clear referents. Preserve who or what each pronoun covers; do not guess when context is missing.
- Preserve negation, quantifiers, and modality. “Some,” “most,” “may,” “must,” “not,” and “not all” are not interchangeable.
- Make coordinated list items grammatically parallel when they carry equal weight.
- Join two independent clauses with a period, a semicolon, or a comma plus a coordinating conjunction; do not join them with a comma alone.
- Review repeated transitions and generic signposting where they obscure the actual relation. Keep “however,” “therefore,” and similar words when they express a real contrast or inference. Research on metadiscourse varies by genre and prompt; it does not establish current model fingerprints.
- Follow the requested punctuation style. Without a supplied style, prefer periods, commas, colons, or parentheses over em/en dashes; retain dashes in quotations, ranges, and technical notation.
- Remove unrequested chat or reasoning scaffolding from the deliverable.
- Keep the tone requested. Remove unsolicited hype, reassurance, excessive praise, or snark when it does not fit.
- Keep facts, numbers, citations, and uncertainty grounded in supplied sources. Never invent personal experience or details to make writing seem human.
- Review repetition, stock contrasts, padding, repetitive cadence, and generic wrap-ups. Revise only where they weaken clarity or voice.
- Vary sentence and paragraph rhythm naturally. Do not add errors, fragments, or awkwardness to imitate a person.
- Match the requested format, audience, and register.
- No style rule can guarantee a detector result or prove authorship.
